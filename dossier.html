Module 4 · Geology essentials, just in time

Page 2: Hemi's geology and what it implies

How close was yours?
  • Deposit type: intrusion-hosted Au in diorite intrusions (Mallina Basin, Pilbara Craton)
  • Expectation box: about 1–2 g/t, large tonnage, open pit. The reserve of 1.5 g/t over ~121 Mt fits the box.
  • Cover: transported sediments masked the discovery. Aircore was the right tool.
  • Weathering: there is an oxide and transitional cap, but most ore is fresh sulphide, so expect harder, partly refractory feed.
  • Questions for later: the recovery split between free-milling and refractory ore (Module 8); strip ratio 6.6:1 (Module 9).

Module 9 · Mining methods & infrastructure

Page 7: Mining Hemi

How close was yours?
  • Method: large open pits, strip ratio 6.6:1. Contractor vs owner mining is to be confirmed in NST's redesign.

Module 11 · Operations, reconciliation & ramp-up

Page 9: Hemi ramp-up risk

How close was yours?
  • Flowsheet complexity (flotation + POX + CIL) suggests McNulty Category 2–3, not Category 1. Expect a 12–24 month ramp to nameplate unverified.
  • Year-1 feed: oxide and transitional ore first, then fresh sulphides through the autoclave. Recovery may vary.
  • Under NST, the balance sheet absorbs ramp-up risk, so no equity raise is needed, unlike a standalone De Grey.
  • What to track after FID: monthly autoclave availability, recovery by ore type and NST's guidance revisions.

Module 22 · Management, governance & capital allocation

Page 20: Management and the Hemi outcome

How close was yours?
  • De Grey's team: discovered Hemi under cover, grew the resource, delivered a DFS, raised capital into strength (A$300m with the DFS) and sold to a builder (NST), rather than attempting a A$1.3bn+ build themselves.
  • Was selling right? It crystallised value and removed funding and execution risk, but it gave up the full upside of a higher gold price (partly retained through NST scrip).
  • NST as acquirer: a track record of counter-cyclical, scrip-funded deals. Judge it on Hemi's delivery: FID late FY27, then construction and ramp-up.

Module 24 · Red flags: the 20-point checklist

Page 22: Red-flag audit of De Grey (2020–24)

How close was yours?

Score the early DEG releases honestly:

  • Early downhole widths before orientation was known: minor (normal for early-stage exploration).
  • Funding: frequent raises, but at modest discounts and into strength. Low concern.
  • Management: a credible discovery team, with well-regarded directors and a major shareholder base. Low concern.
  • Technical: refractory metallurgy needing POX (a real risk, disclosed and studied).
  • Typical score: about 2–4. A real discovery with normal risks, not a promotion.
