A study is a DCF someone else built

Every mining study is, at heart, a discounted cash-flow model with a lot of engineering behind the inputs. Your job is to audit the inputs, not admire the outputs. Who built it, at what price, with how much contingency, and with what share of Inferred material?

The study ladder

Stage Typical accuracy AACE class Contingency What it is for
Scoping study (PEA in Canada) ±30–50% Class 5 High or none Is this worth studying? May use Inferred resources (with cautionary statements)
Pre-feasibility (PFS) ±20–25% Class 4 15–25% Choose the option; first Ore Reserve
Definitive / bankable feasibility (DFS/BFS) ±10–15% Class 3 10–15% Basis for financing and FID

What moves between stages

  • Capex usually rises. More engineering reveals more scope: bigger tailings dams, more power, higher-spec plants. Owner's costs and contingency are added. Inflation adds more.
  • Recovery and throughput get firmer after variability testwork.
  • Mine plans tighten: dilution, ore loss, geotechnical slopes.
  • Price decks change with the market and the owner's conservatism.
▣ Case studyHemi: PFS → DFS ✔

DFS capex was 24% above the PFS base estimate (28% including growth allowance and contingency). The DFS headline capex was A$1,298m (including A$162m of growth allowance and contingency) plus A$47m pre-strip = A$1,345m. The DFS targeted first gold in H2 2026. Under NST, FID is now targeted for late FY27 ✔.

▣ Case studyLake Resources: the Kachi reset (Jun 2023) ✔

Lake reset its Kachi lithium-brine project (Argentina): first production slipped from 2024 to 2027, phase-1 output was halved to 25 ktpa, and the capex estimate rose from about US$544m to US$1.1–1.5bn. The shares fell about 43% that month. An early study on a novel process (direct lithium extraction) carries wide error bars.

Reading a study: the checklist

  1. Price deck vs spot vs consensus. Is the study at a conservative long-term price or at spot?
  2. Discount rate, and real vs nominal. Most studies are real (no inflation) at 5–8%.
  3. Inferred share of the production target.
  4. Capex build-up: direct, indirect, owner's costs, contingency, growth allowance, pre-strip, working capital. What is excluded?
  5. Execution model: EPCM (the owner bears overrun risk) or EPC/lump-sum (the contractor bears it, at a price).
  6. Ramp-up assumption: month 1 at 100%? (Unrealistic; see Module 11.)
  7. Sensitivities: which input moves NPV most? (Module 17.)
  8. Taxes and royalties included correctly?

Why DFS capex rises (and keeps rising)

An analyst's prior should be that DFS capex will be exceeded in construction, especially for complex flowsheets, remote sites or inflationary periods unverified. Module 17 makes a 25% capex overrun one of the standard stress tests.

◎ Worked exampleRebuild a headline NPV (quick check)

Hemi DFS: average 530 koz/yr for 10 years at A$2,700 with AISC A$1,295 → annual margin ≈ 0.53 × (2,700 − 1,295) ≈ A$745m. Ten years of A$745m at 5% has a PV of about A$5.75bn. Subtract capex A$1.345bn and add a tail of about A$0.2bn → ≈ A$4.6bn, close to the A$4.2bn pre-tax headline. The gap reflects timing (two years of construction before the margin), discounting from FID and the lower-margin tail. Build it properly in M16 · DCF, NPV & NAV.

  • Price deck A$2,700 (conservative then; very conservative now).
  • Capex A$1,345m, +24% vs PFS. Execution model and owner's costs need checking. Inflation since 2023 is a risk.

⚑ Red flagStudy red flags
  • Scoping studies with high Inferred share headlined as if bankable
  • Capex well below peers' capital intensity
  • Nameplate production from month 1
  • A "growth allowance" removed between drafts
  • Owner's costs, working capital or closure omitted
  • Study prices at spot after a big rally
