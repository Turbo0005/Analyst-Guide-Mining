Why one spine case?

Learning science is clear that concepts stick when they hang on a story. De Grey Mining's Hemi in WA's Pilbara is our story. Its arc is closed: discovery in 2020, a DFS in 2023 and a takeover in 2025. So the core numbers will not go stale, and the arc shows almost every concept in this guide. A live epilogue (approvals in 2026, FID targeted for late FY27) shows that projects run late even after the takeover.

Act I: the discovery (Feb 2020)

De Grey was a long-suffering Pilbara gold explorer with a small resource at Mallina. In early 2020 it announced a new discovery, named Hemi, south of Port Hedland unverified. Aircore and RC holes hit broad zones of gold mineralisation beneath transported cover, sand and gravel that had hidden it from earlier explorers.

▣ Case study6 February 2020

DEG shares rose about 115% intraday to 9.9c on the discovery release ✔. Hemi was large, shallow, near infrastructure (a highway, gas and a port) and in a top-tier jurisdiction. The market correctly sensed a company-maker.

What made it special? A first-class analyst would have asked the questions that Modules 4–5 teach:

  • Geology: an intrusion-hosted system in Archaean diorite and quartz-diorite intrusions in the Mallina Basin. It is not a textbook intrusion-related gold system, and some lodes are shear-controlled ✔.
  • Intercepts: broad widths at shallow depth, which suggested a bulk open pit.
  • Metallurgy: sulphide-hosted and partly refractory, which became a key study issue (Module 8).

Act II: growth, studies and money (2020–2023)

Resource drilling expanded Hemi rapidly. Each resource update fed a bigger study, and each study needed equity. That is the orphan period of Module 2, softened here by spectacular resource growth.

◎ Worked exampleThe Definitive Feasibility Study: 28 Sep 2023 ✔
  • Ore Reserve: 6.0 Moz @ 1.5 g/t (about 121 Mt)
  • Plant: 10 Mtpa; strip ratio 6.6:1; 12-year evaluation period
  • Production: 553 koz/yr at an AISC of A$1,229/oz in years 1–5; 530 koz/yr at A$1,295/oz over years 1–10; peak about 570 koz
  • Capex: A$1,298m (including A$162m growth allowance and contingency) + A$47m pre-strip = A$1,345m
  • Economics at A$2,700/oz: NPV5 A$4.2bn pre-tax / A$2.9bn post-tax; IRR 45% / 36%; payback 1.5 / 1.8 years
  • Timing: first gold targeted for H2 2026
  • Funding: a A$300m placement announced alongside it

Notice three things that you will learn to spot:

  1. The price deck. A$2,700/oz was conservative against spot, even in 2023. By Oct 2026, A$ gold was ≈A$5,750 as of 29 Oct 2026. The NPV at spot is several times larger, as you will calculate in Module 16.
  2. The capex trend. DFS capex was 24% above the PFS base estimate (28% including growth allowance and contingency) ✔. Capex rises from study to study more often than not (Module 15).
  3. The placement. Money was raised on the good news, because it was available. That is classic developer financing (Module 14).

Act III: the takeover (Dec 2024 – May 2025)

▣ Case studyNorthern Star bids: 2 Dec 2024 ✔
  • Terms: 0.119 NST shares for each DEG share, all scrip
  • Implied value: A$2.08 per DEG share, a 37.1% premium to the A$1.52 close
  • Market reaction: DEG closed up 29.6% at A$1.97, a spread of about 5% to the offer value
  • Completion: 5 May 2025, at a value of about A$5bn

From about 4.6c before the discovery to A$2.08 is more than a 40-fold rise in under five years, before allowing for dilution along the way. Northern Star paid for a de-risked, permitted-to-be, fully studied project in its home state. De Grey's shareholders took NST scrip and kept exposure to the upside.

Epilogue: projects still run late

The DFS targeted first gold in H2 2026. Under Northern Star, Hemi received WA EPA approval in May 2026, and FID is targeted for late FY27, according to NST's September 2026 quarterly ✔. NST also restated the numbers: a 13.2 Moz resource and 5.5 Moz reserve as at 31 Mar 2026 ✔. The resource went up and the reserve went down, a lesson about owners, assumptions and dates that Module 6 unpacks.

Three questions to answer by the end of the guide:

  1. What was Hemi worth at spot gold, compared with the A$2.9bn DFS NPV? (Module 16)
  2. Was ≈A$5bn a fair price per ounce? (Module 18)
  3. What could still go wrong between now and steady-state production? (Modules 11, 15, 21)
⟳ What happened next?The day of the bid

NST offered A$2.08. Where did DEG close? Before you check, think about why a target trades below a scrip offer. (DEG closed at A$1.97. The gap is the merger-arbitrage spread, the price of time and deal risk. See Module 23.)
