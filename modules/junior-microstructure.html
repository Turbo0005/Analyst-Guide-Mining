The plumbing behind small-cap moves

Junior mining stocks often move for reasons that have nothing to do with geology: a placement, a stale holder exiting, an index change, the June tax-loss season or a promoter's newsletter. Learning the plumbing stops you from mistaking flows for fundamentals.

Placement capacity and the raise cycle

  • LR 7.1: an entity may issue up to 15% of its capital in 12 months without shareholder approval.
  • LR 7.1A: eligible smaller entities can seek approval at the AGM for an extra 10%.
  • The typical cycle: good news (or a looming cash shortfall) → trading halt → lead manager runs a bookbuild over 1–2 days → placement announced at a discount → shares resume → an SPP may follow for retail.
  • Lead-manager fees: typically 5–6% of funds raised plus options unverified. They create an incentive to raise more often.
⚠ Common trapSelling into the placement

Placement participants often sell part of their allocation when trading resumes, especially if they received free attaching options. That puts pressure on the price in the days after a raise.

Liquidity and position size

∑ Key formulaDays to exit

Days to exit = position value ÷ (average daily value traded × the share of daily volume you can take)

Taking more than 20–25% of daily volume moves the price against you unverified. And liquidity evaporates exactly when bad news arrives.

Registers, escrow and overhangs

  • Top-20 register: published in the annual report. A tight register (top 20 > 60%) means low free float, so the stock can move sharply on small volumes. Check for strategic holders, funds and directors.
  • Substantial holders (≥5%): must file notices. Watch for funds building or exiting.
  • Escrow: vendor and IPO shares are locked up for 12–24 months. Escrow release dates create supply.
  • Option overhangs: large numbers of options near the share price cap rallies, because holders exercise and sell.

Index and ETF flows

  • S&P/ASX index inclusion (rebalances in March, June, September and December) brings forced buying. Exclusion brings forced selling.
  • GDX/GDXJ (VanEck's gold-miner ETFs) hold many ASX gold names. Flows into the ETFs buy the constituents, regardless of fundamentals.

The calendar

  • June: tax-loss selling. Australian investors crystallise losses before 30 June, which pressures losers. Some rebound in July.
  • Diggers & Dealers (Kalgoorlie, August), PDAC (Toronto, March), RIU and Mines and Money conferences: announcements cluster around them.
  • Quarterly reporting (late January, April, July, October): a cluster of cash and production news.
  • AGM season (October–November): 7.1A approvals and remuneration votes.

Price queries, aware letters and promotion

  • Price queries: the ASX asks a company to explain an unusual price move. "No explanation" answers are common. Repeated queries are a warning.
  • Aware letters: the ASX asks when a company became aware of information, i.e. was disclosure late?
  • Paid promotion: "sponsored" newsletter and social-media coverage. The disclosure is often in fine print.
  • HotCopper sentiment: extreme forum enthusiasm can be a contrary indicator unverified.
✦ Hemi dossierPage 18: DEG trading dynamics
  • Discovery spike: +115% intraday (Feb 2020) on a modest share count; liquidity exploded.
  • Placements and SPPs funded the growth. The A$300m placement (Sep 2023) brought in large institutions.
  • Index inclusion as DEG grew increased institutional ownership and lowered volatility.
  • Bid day: DEG traded heavily at A$1.97 against the A$2.08 offer as arbitrage funds replaced long-term holders (Module 23).
⚑ Red flagMicrostructure red flags
  • Regular raises with attaching options to the same "sophisticated investors"
  • Large escrow releases or option expiries around "good news"
  • Price-query responses with no explanation, repeatedly
  • Paid promotion clustered before a placement
