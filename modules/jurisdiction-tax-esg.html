Jurisdiction is a cash-flow issue

Analysts often handle "country risk" by bumping up the discount rate. That misses the point. Jurisdiction risk usually arrives as specific cash-flow events: a royalty hike, a tax claim, an export ban, a permit delay, a forced renegotiation or a closure order. Model the cash flows first, then decide whether a risk factor is still needed.

Australia: royalties and tax

◆ Key ideaWestern Australian royalty rates ✔
Commodity Royalty
Gold 2.5% of royalty value (first 2,500 oz a year exempt)
Lithium (spodumene concentrate) 5%
Iron ore 7.5% (crushed and screened, i.e. fines and lump) or 5% (beneficiated)
  • Company tax: 30% (lower for small base-rate entities). Carried-forward tax losses are a NAV asset (Bellevue had more than A$430m ✔).
  • MRRT history: the Minerals Resource Rent Tax on iron ore and coal (2012–14) was repealed after raising little. It is a reminder that fiscal regimes change, even in tier-1 countries.
  • Other states have their own royalty schedules (Queensland's coal royalty tiers, for example).

Permits, native title and heritage

  • WA EPA (state environmental approval) and the federal EPBC Act for matters of national significance. Hemi received WA EPA approval in May 2026 ✔.
  • Native title agreements with Traditional Owners are required for mining tenure and are increasingly a partnership.
  • Cultural heritage: the destruction of the Juukan Gorge rock shelters by Rio Tinto in 2020 led to the exit of the CEO and two senior executives ✔ and to new heritage laws and scrutiny.

ESG as cash flows and liabilities

  • Tailings: the Global Industry Standard on Tailings Management (GISTM), introduced after the Brumadinho disaster, sets expectations for large dams. A tailings failure is existential.
  • Water: in arid WA and Chile, water access can cap production.
  • Closure: rehabilitation costs are a real liability (Module 7). They are often underestimated and rise over time.
  • Carbon: diesel and power emissions are increasingly priced (Safeguard Mechanism for large Australian emitters).
◎ Worked exampleConverting a score into adjustments

A project in a mid-ranked jurisdiction scores 45/100.

  • Cash-flow adjustments first: add a 2% higher royalty from year 5 (a probability-weighted policy change), a 12-month permitting delay and a 10% capex contingency for security and logistics.
  • Then a risk factor: 0.7× on the residual NPV, instead of 1.0× for WA. Explicit cash-flow adjustments make the risk debatable. A vague 15% discount rate does not.

  • WA, Pilbara: tier-1 jurisdiction, stable royalty (2.5% gold, first 2,500 oz exempt), 30% tax.

  • Score: high on fiscal stability, permitting clarity and infrastructure. Main risk: permitting time, not outcome.
⚑ Red flagJurisdiction red flags
  • New mining codes or royalty reviews announced
  • Coups, elections with resource-nationalist platforms
  • Government "carried interests" increasing
  • Projects depending on export permits for raw ore
  • Unresolved native-title or heritage issues
