Producers live and die by guidance

Once a mine is running, the market judges it every quarter against guidance: production and cost ranges for the financial year. Beats are rewarded modestly. Misses, especially repeated ones, are punished hard, because each miss says something about the reliability of the reserve, the plant or management.

Diagnose the downgrade

Ounces = tonnes × grade × recovery, so every production miss comes from one of those three. A cost miss can come on top.

Symptom Likely cause Severity
Grade below plan Reconciliation problem (the model over-predicts), dilution, mine sequencing Most serious: it may mean the reserve is overstated
Throughput below plan Harder ore, mill availability, mining-rate shortfall, water or power Often fixable with capital or time
Recovery below plan Ore-type change (transitional or fresh), refractory ore, plant issues Serious if structural
Costs above plan Inflation, contractor rates, lower volumes spreading fixed costs Usually cyclical, but compounding

Reconciliation: model → grade control → mill

Reconciliation compares what the resource model predicted, what grade control predicted and what the mill actually received. Persistent negative reconciliation (fewer ounces than the model) is a red flag about the resource estimate itself. Good companies publish reconciliation. Evasive ones talk about "sequencing".

Ramp-up curves

New plants rarely hit nameplate on day one.

? UnverifiedThe McNulty curves

A classic 1998 study by McNulty grouped ramp-ups into four categories. Category 1 (proven technology, thorough testwork) reaches nameplate within about a year. Category 4 (novel flowsheets, poor testwork) may never reach it. Many plants take 12–36 months to reach nameplate unverified.

◆ Key ideaRamp-up and funding

A ramp-up shortfall is a cash hole exactly when debt repayments begin and the balance sheet is weakest. That is why ramp-up problems so often end in an equity raise, frequently at a deep discount, after a trading halt.

▣ Case studyBellevue Gold (ASX: BGL), 2025 ✔
  • FY25 guidance was first 165–180 koz. It was cut to 150–165 koz (Jan 2025), then to 129–134 koz (Apr 2025).
  • After a trading suspension, Bellevue raised A$156.5m at 85c, a 25.8% discount to the A$1.145 last trade.
  • The shares fell as much as 26.6% on 15 Apr 2025.
  • About 152 koz of hedges at ≈A$2,843/oz remained, far below spot.

Every element of this module appears here: serial downgrades, a ramp-up cash hole, a discounted raise and a hedge book that limited the upside.

▣ Case studyLiontown's Kathleen Valley

Liontown began underground stoping in Apr 2025, ceased open-pit mining in Dec 2025 and is ramping up towards 2.8 Mtpa ✔. The transition from pit to underground during a lithium-price slump shows two ramp-up risks stacked: technical and cyclical.

Tracking guidance credibility

Build a simple table for every producer you follow: guidance at the start of the year, any revisions, and the actual result. Over 3–5 years a pattern emerges. Some management teams guide conservatively and beat. Others guide aggressively and miss. Apply a credibility haircut to the forecasts of the latter in your valuation.

✎ Your turnDiagnose this quarter

"Production of 48 koz was below expectations owing to lower head grade (1.31 g/t vs 1.52 g/t planned) as mining sequencing delayed access to higher-grade stopes. Mill throughput was a record 1.25 Mt."

What is the likely cause, how worried should you be, and what would you check next quarter? (Grade, possibly sequencing. Check whether grade recovers next quarter and whether reconciliation is disclosed. Repeated misses would point to the model.)

⚑ Red flagOperations red flags
  • Serial guidance downgrades (two or more in one year)
  • A trading halt or suspension followed by a "capital raising"
  • Grade misses blamed on "sequencing" several quarters running
  • No reconciliation disclosure
  • Nameplate claimed on a single good month
