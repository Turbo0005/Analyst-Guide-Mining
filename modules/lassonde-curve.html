The curve in one picture

Pierre Lassonde, co-founder of Franco-Nevada, popularised a chart that every resources analyst carries in their head. It traces the typical value path of a successful discovery, from a concept to a producing mine.

The shape has five parts:

  1. Concept and exploration. Low value and high hope. The company is valued on its ground, its team and the commodity theme.
  2. The discovery spike. A great hole re-rates the stock several-fold in days. Speculators pour in.
  3. The orphan period. Resource drilling, metallurgy, environmental baselines and studies take years. Speculators leave because news-flow slows. Institutions are not yet interested because there is no cash flow. Dilution continues. The share price sags, often for years.
  4. Development and financing. A DFS, permits and a funding package arrive. Construction risk peaks, and the stock re-rates as risk falls.
  5. The production re-rate. Cash flow arrives and generalist investors and indices buy. For many ASX developers, the "exit" is a takeover somewhere between steps 4 and 5 (Module 23).
◆ Key ideaThe right side is a different business

The left side of the curve is about geology and hype. The right side is about engineering, money and execution. The skills, the shareholders and often the management change between the two. Many explorers' boards are not the people who should build a A$1bn mine.

Base rates: the numbers promoters leave out

∑ Key formulaDiscovery to production

S&P Global Market Intelligence studied 127 mines that started production in 2002–23. They took an average of 15.7 years from discovery to production, with a range of 6–32 years ✔.

Promoters' presentations routinely show "first production in 3 years". Treat any such claim as a best case. Then ask what the base rate implies for funding needs, dilution and NPV.

? UnverifiedHow many explorers ever become mines?

There is no reliable single statistic, but the direction is clear. Only a small fraction of exploration companies ever make a discovery, and a smaller fraction of discoveries become mines unverified. Size explorer positions as if most will fail. Module 27 turns this into stage-based position caps.

Stage risk-weighting: an introduction

Analysts translate the curve into numbers by risk-weighting a project's NPV according to its stage. Module 16 formalises this. For now, the idea is that the same NPV is worth very different amounts at different stages.

Stage Illustrative share of NPV the market pays
Exploration target / early resource 0.1–0.3×
PFS complete 0.3–0.5×
DFS + permits, unfunded 0.4–0.6×
Funded, in construction 0.6–0.8×
Producing, steady state 0.8–1.0×+

unverified

The re-rating between stages is the opportunity. The orphan period is where patient, well-researched investors buy de-risking that the market is too bored to price.

Reading the real pins

  • De Grey (DEG) went through the textbook path. The discovery spike came in Feb 2020 (+115% intraday). Resource growth followed, then the DFS and a A$300m placement in Sep 2023, then a takeover in Dec 2024 before construction. Shareholders were paid for de-risking that had not happened yet.
  • Chalice (CHN) spiked and then entered a long orphan period. Its studies were slowed by low PGM and nickel prices. Its stock is now about 91% below its 2021 peak (Oct 2026) ✔.
  • Liontown (LTR) went from bid target (A$3.00 proposal, Oct 2023) to a heavily diluted developer, to a producer ramping up into a falling lithium price ✔.
  • Paladin (PDN) shows a second production re-rate. Langer Heinrich in Namibia restarted, with commercial production declared on 30 Mar 2024 ✔.
  • Northern Star (NST) sits on the right-hand side: a producer and consolidator.
⟳ What happened next?Predict before you scroll

In Feb 2020, De Grey announced Hemi. Where on the curve was it the day before, and what kind of event would re-rate it next? (Answer: deep in the exploration stage. The re-rating event was the discovery itself, and DEG rose about 115% intraday.)

⚑ Red flagPromoting the orphan period away

"We will go straight from discovery to production in two years by toll-treating" is sometimes true for small, high-grade gold deposits near an existing mill. For anything that needs its own plant, it is almost always fantasy.
