The people who spend your money

In mining, management decides where billions of dollars of capital go: which projects to build, when to raise, whom to buy and when to sell. The same orebody can be a triumph or a disaster depending on who runs it.

Builders vs promoters

Builders Promoters
A track record of building mines on time and on budget A track record of raising money and pivoting
Under-promise, over-deliver on guidance Headline-first releases, "up to" language
Hire strong technical teams and use independent experts Lean on paid promotion and newsletters
Raise when they need to, at sensible discounts Raise constantly, with free options
Talk about per-share value Talk about market cap and "the story"

Alignment

  • Insider ownership: meaningful on-market buying by directors with their own money is a good signal. Options granted for free are not alignment.
  • Remuneration hurdles: reward per-share metrics (NAV per share, reserves per share, FCF per share, TSR vs peers), not production growth or market cap, which can be bought with dilution.
  • Related parties: consulting contracts with directors' firms, leases from insiders and acquisitions from associates. Check 5B item 6 and the annual report.

Capital allocation through the cycle

  • Returns on capital: measure over a full cycle. Peak-cycle ROCE flatters everyone.
  • Buybacks vs growth: buybacks at peak prices destroy value. Buybacks in downturns can create it.
  • Counter-cyclical M&A: the best acquirers buy in downturns, with scrip when their own shares are richly valued, or cash when targets are cheap. The worst buy at the top with cash.
▣ Case studyMinRes governance (Nov 2024) ✔

Mineral Resources' board review of governance failures led to the announcement that founder and managing director Chris Ellison would step down. About A$8.8m of pay was forfeited, plus up to A$9.6m more. Lesson: even founder-led companies with great operating records can carry governance risk, and the market discounts it.

▣ Case studySandfire–MATSA ✔

Sandfire Resources acquired the MATSA copper operations in Spain for US$1.865bn (effective 1 Feb 2022), transforming itself from a single-mine company (DeGrussa, approaching closure) into a multi-asset producer. It is a case study in reserve-replacement M&A: necessary, large and only judged over years.

✦ Hemi dossierPage 20: Management and the Hemi outcome
  • De Grey's team: discovered Hemi under cover, grew the resource, delivered a DFS, raised capital into strength (A$300m with the DFS) and sold to a builder (NST), rather than attempting a A$1.3bn+ build themselves.
  • Was selling right? It crystallised value and removed funding and execution risk, but it gave up the full upside of a higher gold price (partly retained through NST scrip).
  • NST as acquirer: a track record of counter-cyclical, scrip-funded deals. Judge it on Hemi's delivery: FID late FY27, then construction and ramp-up.
⚑ Red flagManagement red flags
  • Directors selling on market around good news
  • Remuneration tied to production or market-cap growth alone
  • Related-party consulting fees large relative to exploration spend
  • Serial pivots (gold → lithium → uranium → AI)
  • Board with no independent technical director
