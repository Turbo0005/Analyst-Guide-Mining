One checklist to rule them all

Every module so far has ended with red flags. This module consolidates them into a single weighted checklist. Use it as a filter. Use it before you fall in love with a story, not after.

The 20 points, grouped

Disclosure (Modules 5–6)

  1. Headline intercepts with no true-width statement
  2. "Up to" and "potential" language without a JORC resource
  3. Metal equivalents without prices, recoveries or component grades, or dominated by by-products
  4. Rock chips or soils headlined as discoveries
  5. Exploration Targets in headlines; stale estimates re-quoted under LR 5.23

Strategy and management (Modules 22–23)

  1. Pivots to hot commodities without expertise
  2. Management with failed or delisted companies
  3. Directors selling on market
  4. Related-party transactions and consultancies
  5. "Strategic review" after a trading halt with no process

Funding (Modules 7, 14, 20)

  1. Fewer than two quarters of cash
  2. Overheads exceeding exploration spend
  3. Frequent deep-discount raises
  4. Toxic convertibles or option resets
  5. Paid promotion

Technical and valuation (Modules 8–11, 15–17)

  1. Study capex well below peer capital intensity
  2. Recovery assumptions without variability testwork
  3. Price decks well above consensus
  4. Serial guidance downgrades; a suspension before a raise

Governance (Module 22)

  1. Auditor changes, qualified opinions, ASX aware letters or price queries

Two cases

▣ Case studyBre-X (1993–97) ✔

Claims of 70 Moz up to as much as 200 Moz at Busang. Salted samples confirmed by Strathcona (1997). Peak value ≈C$6bn, then zero. The red flags at the time: no independent assays, crushed core, extraordinary claims and limited QA/QC transparency. It led to NI 43-101 (Feb 2001).

▣ Case studyLake Resources (2022–23) ✔

A short-seller report in Jul 2022 questioned Lake's timelines and its technology partner. In Jun 2023 Lake reset Kachi: production delayed from 2024 to 2027, phase-1 output halved to 25 ktpa and capex up from ≈US$544m to US$1.1–1.5bn. The shares fell ≈43% that month. Red flags that were visible: a novel process (DLE) on an aggressive timeline, and a study-stage capex far below eventual estimates.

How to use the score

  • 0–3: normal noise. Investigate the specific flags.
  • 4–7: high caution. Require a higher margin of safety and smaller size.
  • 8+: walk away, unless you have a specific, evidence-backed reason the market is wrong.

✦ Hemi dossierPage 22: Red-flag audit of De Grey (2020–24)

Score the early DEG releases honestly:

  • Early downhole widths before orientation was known: minor (normal for early-stage exploration).
  • Funding: frequent raises, but at modest discounts and into strength. Low concern.
  • Management: a credible discovery team, with well-regarded directors and a major shareholder base. Low concern.
  • Technical: refractory metallurgy needing POX (a real risk, disclosed and studied).
  • Typical score: about 2–4. A real discovery with normal risks, not a promotion.
