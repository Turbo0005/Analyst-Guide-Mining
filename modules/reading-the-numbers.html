The documents an analyst lives in

Document Who lodges it When What to read first
Quarterly activities report Most ASX miners Within a month of quarter end Production, costs, guidance, cash movement
Appendix 5B Mining exploration entities (explorers and developers) Quarterly, with the activities report Cash burn, item 6, funded quarters (section 8)
Half-year and annual accounts All listed companies Aug/Feb (June year-end) Cash-flow statement, notes on provisions, impairments, stockpiles
Resource / reserve statements Typically annually Varies Date, owner, price deck, cut-off
ℹ Note5B, not 4C

Mining exploration entities lodge the Appendix 5B quarterly cash-flow report under ASX Listing Rule 5.5. The Appendix 4C is the equivalent for certain other entity types, not for miners.

Producer KPIs: the chain from mine to cash

∑ Key formulaThe production chain

Ore milled (t) × head grade (g/t) × recovery (%) ÷ 31.1035 = gold produced (oz)

Then: produced → sold (inventory changes) × realised price (hedges!) = revenue.

Watch each link:

  • Ore mined vs milled. If mined < milled, the company is drawing down stockpiles, which flatters costs for a while.
  • Head grade vs reserve grade. Persistent grade below the reserve grade suggests a reconciliation problem (Module 11).
  • Recovery. A falling recovery may mean harder or more refractory ore.
  • Produced vs sold. Gold in circuit and unsold doré move revenue between quarters.
  • Realised vs spot. A realised price well below spot usually means hedges (Module 19).

AISC vs actual cash flow

AISC is an industry-standard cost metric (WGC, Module 10). It is not free cash flow. It excludes growth capital, exploration on new projects, interest, tax, and often working-capital movements.

∑ Key formulaFree cash flow per ounce

FCF/oz = (operating cash flow − all capex − lease payments) ÷ ounces sold

Compare FCF/oz with (realised price − AISC). A persistent gap means costs are hiding outside AISC.

◎ Worked exampleWorked: rebuild a fictional quarter (Goldfield Co.)
Item Value
Ore milled 1.20 Mt @ 1.60 g/t, 91% recovery
Gold sold 56,000 oz @ A$5,600 realised
Site cash costs (mining, processing, G&A) A$128m
Royalties A$8.0m
Sustaining capex + capitalised stripping A$32m
Corporate G&A, sustaining leases A$6m
Growth capex (new underground) A$40m
Operating cash flow (after tax and interest) A$150m
  1. Produced = 1.2m × 1.6 × 0.91 ÷ 31.1035 ≈ 56,170 oz, consistent with sales of 56,000.
  2. AISC = (128 + 8 + 32 + 6) ÷ 56,000 = A$3,107/oz. AISC margin = 5,600 − 3,107 = A$2,493/oz.
  3. FCF = 150 − 32 − 40 − (leases already in OCF? check the note) ≈ A$78m, or about A$1,390/oz.

The gap between A$2,493 and A$1,390 is tax, interest, growth capex and working capital. A good analyst explains it rather than ignoring it.

✎ Your turnFaded

The next quarter: 1.25 Mt @ 1.45 g/t, 90% recovery; site costs A$131m; royalties A$7.5m; sustaining capex A$35m; corporate A$6m. Compute ounces produced and AISC per ounce (assume sold = produced). (Answer: ≈52,450 oz; AISC ≈ A$3,423/oz.) What happened to the margin, and which KPI caused it?

The Appendix 5B for explorers and developers

The 5B is a standardised cash-flow statement that every mining exploration entity lodges each quarter. Three sections matter most:

  • Operating and investing cash flows. How much went into exploration and evaluation versus staff and admin.
  • Item 6: payments to related parties (directors' fees, salaries, consulting firms owned by directors). An explanation is required.
  • Section 8: estimated quarters of funding available. If it is below 2, the company must answer further questions about how it will fund itself, a strong signal that a raise is coming.
Go deeper: Accounting traps in mining optional
Item What happens Why it matters
Provisional pricing Concentrate sales are marked to forward prices until final settlement Revenue swings with commodity prices after the quarter
Stockpiles and NRV write-downs Low-grade stockpiles are carried at cost and written down if price × recovery falls Can create large non-cash charges
Capitalised stripping and development Waste removal and underground development go on the balance sheet Lowers reported costs today and raises depreciation tomorrow
Rehabilitation provisions The present value of closure costs, with an unwinding interest charge A real liability, often underestimated
Impairments Write-downs when value < book Signals that management's own NPV has fallen
Stream deferred revenue Upfront stream cash is booked as a liability and recognised over deliveries Revenue looks higher than cash received later
Hedge accounting Mark-to-market gains and losses go through reserves or P&L Earnings can swing without any cash moving
Tax losses Carried-forward losses shelter future profits A real asset in NAV (Bellevue had more than A$430m ✔)

Listing Rules Chapter 5: the disclosure rules you will cite

  • LR 5.7–5.8: exploration results and resource reports must follow JORC with Table 1 and the CP's consent.
  • LR 5.16–5.19: production targets and forecast financial information must disclose material assumptions, the share of Inferred resources and cautionary statements.
  • LR 5.23: re-quoting earlier estimates (Module 6).
✦ Hemi dossierPage 5: De Grey's 5B years

As a developer, De Grey lodged quarterly 5Bs throughout 2020–24. The analyst's habit for each 5B:

  1. Cash at quarter end and section 8 funded quarters.
  2. Exploration and evaluation as a share of spend. Hemi was drilling-heavy, which is a good sign.
  3. Item 6 payments: reasonable for the company's size?
  4. Financing inflows: link each to a placement release and its discount (Module 14). The A$300m placement in Sep 2023 shows up as a financing inflow in the following 5B.
⚑ Red flagNumbers red flags
  • AISC margins that never show up in cash
  • Mined < milled for several quarters (stockpile drawdown flattering costs)
  • Head grade persistently below reserve grade
  • Growing "growth" capex at a mature mine (sustaining capital relabelled)
  • Fewer than 2 funded quarters, or item 6 that is large relative to exploration
