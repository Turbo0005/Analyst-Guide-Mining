Why macro matters more than you would like

For a producer, the commodity price explains most of the share-price variance. For an explorer, the cycle decides whether anyone will fund the next drill programme. You do not have to forecast macro. You do have to know which macro variables your stock is exposed to, and how much.

The AUD: Australia's natural hedge

Commodities are priced in US$. Australian miners pay most of their costs in A$. When commodity prices fall, the AUD tends to fall too (Australia's terms of trade weaken), which cushions A$ revenue.

∑ Key formulaA$ gold

A$ gold price = US$ gold price ÷ AUD/USD

As of 29 Oct 2026: ≈US$4,050 ÷ 0.705 ≈ A$5,750/oz ✔.

◎ Worked exampleThe FX cushion

A WA producer has an AISC of A$2,600/oz.

  • Case 1: US$4,050 gold, AUD 0.705 → A$5,745; margin A$3,145.
  • Case 2: gold falls 10% to US$3,645, but the AUD also falls to 0.66 → A$5,523; margin A$2,923. A 10% US$ gold fall cut the margin by only 7%.
  • Case 3: gold falls 10% and the AUD rises to 0.75 → A$4,860; margin A$2,260 (−28%). The hedge can work against you.

Gold: real rates, the dollar and fear

Gold pays no yield, so its opportunity cost is the real interest rate (nominal yield minus inflation). Falling real rates and a weaker US dollar have historically supported gold. Central-bank buying, geopolitical fear and fiscal worries add demand that has, at times, overpowered the real-rate relationship.

▣ Case studyThe 2026 gold path
  • Record: an intraday spot high of ≈US$5,595/oz on 29 Jan 2026. Some data vendors show ≈US$5,608. The LBMA PM fix peaked at US$5,405 ✔. Always say which series you mean.
  • By 29 Oct 2026: ≈US$4,050/oz, about 28% below the peak ✔.
  • Lesson: even in a structural bull market, 25–30% drawdowns happen. Producer valuations built on peak spot prices de-rate quickly.

China and the bulk commodities

China consumes roughly half the world's iron ore, copper, nickel and many other metals. Watch:

  • Credit impulse: new credit as a share of GDP. It leads industrial activity.
  • Property starts: the biggest single driver of steel demand, in long structural decline since 2021.
  • Steel output and export volumes.
  • Policy signals (stimulus, infrastructure, "new energy" priorities) that shift demand from property towards the grid, EVs and solar.

Input costs and the cost of capital

  • Diesel, power and explosives move AISC directly. Diesel can be 10–20% of open-pit costs unverified.
  • Labour in WA is tight and expensive in commodity booms.
  • Interest rates set project-finance costs and the discount rates investors apply (Module 16).

Equities vs commodities

? UnverifiedGold-equity beta

Gold producers have historically moved about 1.5–2× gold on average unverified. Operating leverage (Module 1) explains much of it. Sentiment, fund flows (GDX) and cost inflation explain the rest. Equities often lead the commodity at turning points and lag in the late stages of a rally, when costs catch up.

  • DFS deck A$2,700/oz vs ≈A$5,750 spot as of 29 Oct 2026: a 113% higher price.
  • Each 5c move in the AUD changes A$ gold by ≈A$400/oz at US$4,050.
  • Cost inflation since 2023 (labour, diesel, steel) eats part of the price windfall, so the NPV at spot is not simply the DFS NPV scaled by price.
  • Action: run the Module 16 model at A$4,000 and A$5,750, with AISC +30%.
⚑ Red flagMacro red flags
  • Valuations using peak spot prices as the long-term deck
  • Ignoring the AUD when comparing ASX and North American producers
  • Projects whose cost base is in a strengthening local currency
