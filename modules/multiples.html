Multiples are a cross-check, not a valuation

Multiples compress a company into one number. That is convenient for screening, but dangerous for decisions, because the number hides the very things that create value in mining. Use them to ask "why is this one different?" and then answer with a DCF.

The main multiples

Multiple Formula Best for
P/NAV Market cap ÷ NAV (or price ÷ NAV per share) All stages; the industry's core multiple
EV/resource oz (or EV/t Cu, EV/t LCE) Enterprise value ÷ contained metal Explorers and early developers
EV/reserve oz EV ÷ reserve ounces Developers and producers
EV/EBITDA EV ÷ EBITDA Producers
FCF yield FCF ÷ market cap Producers, especially at cycle peaks
P/CF Price ÷ operating cash flow per share Producers
? UnverifiedP/NAV by stage unverified
  • Explorers and scoping-stage: 0.2–0.4× (or valued on EV/oz)
  • Developers (DFS, unfunded): 0.4–0.6×
  • Producers (mid-tier): 0.7–1.0×
  • Senior producers and royalty companies: 1.0–2.5×

Premiums go to scale, liquidity, jurisdiction, reserve life and track record. Always compare at the same price deck and discount rate.

Why EV/oz misleads

An ounce in the ground is not an ounce in the bank. EV per resource ounce ignores:

  • Grade: a 5 g/t underground ounce and a 0.5 g/t heap-leach ounce have very different margins.
  • Metallurgy: refractory ounces need expensive processing.
  • Jurisdiction: a WA ounce vs a Sahel ounce.
  • Capex: an ounce next to an existing mill vs one needing a new A$1bn plant.
  • Confidence: Inferred vs Reserve.
  • Stage and timing: an ounce produced in year 2 vs year 15.
◎ Worked exampleWhat did NST pay for De Grey?

Using ≈A$5bn of deal value ✔:

  • Per resource ounce (11.2 Moz, Nov 2024): ≈ A$446/oz
  • Per reserve ounce (6.0 Moz, Sep 2023 DFS): ≈ A$833/oz
  • Per annual ounce (530 koz/yr): ≈ A$9,400 per annual oz

Strictly, EV = equity value − cash + debt. De Grey held cash after its placements, so the true EV was somewhat below A$5bn unverified. Compare A$446/oz with the A$ gold price of the time: the buyer paid less than a tenth of the gold price per ounce in the ground, for a deposit that still needed A$1.3bn+ of capex, years of construction and a ramp-up.

EV/EBITDA and FCF yield for producers

  • EV/EBITDA captures the balance sheet but ignores capex intensity and reserve life. A mine with 3 years of reserves deserves a much lower multiple than one with 15.
  • FCF yield is the cleanest "what do I get" measure, but it is cyclical. A 15% FCF yield at peak prices may become 0% at mid-cycle prices. Calculate it at a mid-cycle deck as well.
  • Through-cycle earnings: average margins over a full cycle, not the latest quarter.

⚑ Red flagMultiples red flags
  • "Cheapest EV/oz in the sector" with no grade or capex adjustment
  • P/NAV comparisons at different price decks
  • FCF yields calculated at peak spot prices
  • Resource ounces dominated by Inferred or equivalents
