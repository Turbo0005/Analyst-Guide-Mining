From holes to ounces

Drill holes are points. A Mineral Resource is a 3D model that interpolates grade between those points. An Ore Reserve is the part of that model that can be mined and processed profitably under realistic assumptions. The gap between the two is where most valuation errors live.

The codes

Code Where Signatory
JORC Code (2012) Australia/NZ (ASX) Competent Person (CP)
NI 43-101 Canada (TSX/TSX-V) Qualified Person (QP)
S-K 1300 US (SEC filers) Qualified Person

All are aligned through CRIRSCO, so the categories are equivalent. NI 43-101 came into force in Feb 2001, after Bre-X ✔.

ℹ NoteJORC 2026 is coming

A new JORC Code is in final review. A provisional code appeared in Q2 2026, release is targeted for late 2026, and a transition of about 12 months is proposed. It brings tighter "reasonable prospects for eventual economic extraction" (RPEEE) tests and ESG considerations among the modifying factors ✔. Expect some resources to shrink as RPEEE tightens.

The categories

◆ Key ideaResource → Reserve ladder
  • Inferred Resource: grade and continuity implied by limited sampling. It cannot be converted to a Reserve.
  • Indicated Resource: reasonable confidence. It can convert to a Probable Reserve.
  • Measured Resource: high confidence. It can convert to a Proved Reserve (or Probable, if the modifying factors are uncertain).
  • Exploration Target: a range of tonnes and grade with no resource. ASX rules require a cautionary statement next to it.

Modifying factors turn resources into reserves: mining (dilution, ore loss, pit design), metallurgy (recovery), economics (prices, costs), and legal, environmental, social and governmental factors.

⚠ Common trapInferred material in studies

Ore Reserves can never include Inferred material. But scoping studies (and Canadian PEAs) may use Inferred resources in production targets, provided cautionary statements accompany them (ASX LR 5.16–5.19). A scoping study with 60% Inferred in years 1–3 is a hypothesis, not a plan.

Grade-tonnage and the cut-off lever

Every resource is reported above a cut-off grade. Lower the cut-off and you include more low-grade blocks: tonnes go up, average grade goes down, and contained metal usually goes up. That is why companies love lowering cut-offs before a resource update. But metal only creates value if each extra tonne pays for itself.

∑ Key formulaBreak-even cut-off (simplified)

Cut-off ≈ cost per tonne ÷ (price per gram × recovery)

Take a A$60/t mining, processing and G&A cost at A$5,750/oz and 90% recovery. One gram is worth 5,750 ÷ 31.1035 × 0.9 ≈ A$166, so the cut-off ≈ 60 ÷ 166 ≈ 0.36 g/t. Real cut-offs also allow for royalties, sustaining capital and opportunity cost.

◎ Worked exampleA resource "upgrade" that is really a cut-off change

A company reports its resource rising from 2.1 Moz @ 1.4 g/t (0.5 g/t cut-off) to 2.9 Moz @ 1.0 g/t (0.3 g/t cut-off) with no new drilling. Contained ounces rose 38%. But grade fell 29%, and the extra 0.8 Moz sits in material that may not cover its costs at a conservative price. Check the price deck and the RPEEE pit shell behind the lower cut-off.

Dates, owners and LR 5.23

ASX Listing Rule 5.23 lets a company re-quote an earlier resource or reserve if it confirms it is "not aware of any new information" that materially affects it. That is convenient, but it means numbers can be years old.

▣ Case studyHemi: a conversion ratio that is not one
  • The DFS reserve of 6.0 Moz (Sep 2023) was based on the June 2023 resource.
  • De Grey's 11.2 Moz resource dates from Nov 2024.
  • Dividing 6.0 by 11.2 gives a meaningless "54% conversion", because they are different models on different dates.
  • Under Northern Star: 13.2 Moz resource and 5.5 Moz reserve at 31 Mar 2026 ✔. A new owner brings its own price deck, cost base and design criteria.

Lesson: always pair resources and reserves from the same date and owner, and read the CP's assumptions.

▣ Case studyBre-X: why the CP signature matters

Bre-X claimed figures from 70 Moz up to as much as 200 Moz, based on salted samples. Independent check assays (Strathcona, 1997) exposed the fraud. Peak market value was about C$6bn ✔. Modern codes put a named, accountable professional's signature on every estimate.

⚑ Red flagResource red flags
  • Cut-off lowered at the same time as a "resource increase"
  • High Inferred share in early production years
  • Resource reported to great depth below any credible pit
  • Re-quoting old estimates under LR 5.23 after a big change in costs
  • Exploration Targets headlined without the cautionary statement
  • Metal-equivalent resources dominated by by-products
