A repeatable research workflow

Expert analysts are not smarter. They are more systematic. This module gives you a workflow you can run every week: screen → tear sheet → deep dive → decision journal.

Step 1: the screening funnel

Filter Example threshold Why
Commodity Gold, copper (your circle of competence) Focus
Stage Developers and small producers Where re-ratings happen
Jurisdiction Australia, Canada, selected others Cash-flow risk (Module 21)
Liquidity ADV > A$500k You can exit (Module 20)

Valuation Implied price below consensus Margin of safety

Data sources

  • Company documents: ASX announcements, SEDAR+ (Canada), EDGAR (US).
  • Prices: LME, CME/COMEX, the Perth Mint; Fastmarkets, Benchmark and SMM for lithium, rare earths and minor metals.
  • Industry: WGC (gold), ICSG (copper), USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries.
  • Broker research: useful for models and peer tables, but read it critically.

Step 2: the tear sheet

Fill this in for each shortlisted company. Keep it to one page, and update it every quarter.

Step 3: the deep dive

Document reading order:

  1. The latest quarterly and 5B (current reality)
  2. The latest resource and reserve statements (with dates)
  3. The latest study (assumptions, sensitivities)
  4. Annual report notes (provisions, related parties, register)
  5. Presentations (last, because they are marketing)

Then:

  • Rebuild the NPV (Module 16) and the implied price (Module 17).
  • Build a funding forecast (Module 14).
  • Score jurisdiction, management and red flags (Modules 21, 22, 24).
  • Channel checks: contractors, suppliers, ex-employees, site visits and industry conferences.

Reading broker research critically

⚠ Common trapBroker notes are not neutral
  • Corporate-finance conflicts: a broker that ran the company's last placement is rarely negative. Check the disclosures at the end of the note.
  • Price decks: check whether the broker's deck is at spot, consensus or their own house view.
  • Risk factors: brokers may apply generous risk factors to developers they finance.
  • Use them for: peer tables, model structure and management access, not for conclusions.

Step 4: scenarios, variant perception and the pre-mortem

∑ Key formulaScenario-weighted value

Expected value = Σ (probability × value in scenario)

Example: bull A$2.40 (25%), base A$1.60 (50%), bear A$0.70 (25%) → A$1.575. Compare it with the price, and also look at the spread. A wide spread means a smaller position.

  • Variant perception: what do you believe that the market does not, and why? Base it on base rates and the implied price, not hope.
  • Pre-mortem: imagine it is two years later and the investment has lost 50%. Write down the three most likely reasons. Then monitor those specifically.
  • Catalyst calendar: drill results, studies, permits, FID, first production, quarterlies, index rebalances.
