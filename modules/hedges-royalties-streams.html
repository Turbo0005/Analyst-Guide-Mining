Between the asset and the equity

A project's NPV is not all yours as a shareholder. Hedges, royalties, streams and offtakes take slices before cash reaches equity holders. Some are good trades (they enabled funding). Others quietly transfer value away. This module teaches you to find them and price them.

Hedging

Instrument What it does Cost
Forward sale Locks in a price for future delivery Gives up all upside
Put option Floor price; keeps the upside Premium paid upfront
Collar Buy a put, sell a call: floor and ceiling Often zero-cost; caps the upside
Gold loan / prepayment Cash now, repaid in ounces Like debt plus a forward
∑ Key formulaHedge mark-to-market

MTM = hedged ounces × (hedge price − spot)

A negative MTM is not a cash loss today. It is revenue forgone as hedges deliver. In NAV, deduct the present value of the MTM. Watch for delivery risk: if production falls short, the company must still deliver into the hedges (or close them out at a loss).

▣ Case studyBellevue Gold's hedge book ✔

Bellevue carried about 152 koz of hedges at ≈A$2,843/oz into its April 2025 guidance cut and raise. With spot A$ gold far higher (≈A$5,750 by Oct 2026 as of 29 Oct 2026), each hedged ounce forgoes about A$2,900. On 152 koz, that is roughly A$440m of revenue forgone at Oct-2026 prices (illustrative: some of those hedges will have been delivered since). Lenders often require hedging for project debt. The lesson is to size hedges to reliable production, not nameplate.

Royalties

Type Basis Who benefits
Gross revenue royalty (GRR) % of revenue The holder: simplest, no cost risk
Net smelter return (NSR) royalty % of revenue after smelting, refining and transport Most common
Net profit interest (NPI) % of profit The operator: holder only paid when profitable
Government royalty Statutory (e.g. WA gold 2.5%) The state (Module 21)

Streams

A stream is an upfront deposit paid to a miner in exchange for the right to buy a share of future production (often a by-product such as gold or silver) at a fixed, low ongoing price (e.g. 10–30% of spot) unverified. It is cheaper than equity for the miner at the time, but it is a permanent claim on production. In NAV, deduct the PV of (spot − ongoing price) × streamed ounces.

Royalty and streaming companies

Franco-Nevada, Wheaton Precious Metals and Royal Gold (NYSE/TSX) and Deterra Royalties (ASX; its core asset is the Mining Area C iron-ore royalty from BHP) own portfolios of royalties and streams. They have no operating cost risk, no capex risk and exposure to exploration upside on others' ground.

? UnverifiedPremium multiples

Royalty companies have historically traded at about 1.5–2.5× NAV unverified, much higher than miners, because of high margins, low risk, diversification and free optionality. The trade-off is lower leverage to price rallies.

Offtakes and prepayments

An offtake is an agreement to sell future production to a buyer (a smelter, converter, trader, automaker or battery maker). It can include a prepayment (cash upfront, repaid in product) or be tied to debt or equity. Treat a prepayment as quasi-debt. Check pricing formulas: a discount to index, floors and caps.

? UnverifiedLithium offtakes

Liontown's offtakes (with Ford, Tesla and LG) and PLS's (with POSCO and Ganfeng) are frequently cited examples unverified. Read the latest company disclosures for pricing mechanisms and any changes.

  • Third-party royalties and streams: check De Grey's disclosures and the NST bidder's statement. Any private royalty over parts of the tenure reduces NPV directly unverified.

⚑ Red flagEncumbrance red flags
  • Hedges above 30–40% of near-term production for a mine still ramping up
  • Streams on the main metal, not a by-product
  • Multiple stacked royalties over the core ore
  • Offtake prepayments counted as "non-dilutive funding" with no debt-like disclosure
