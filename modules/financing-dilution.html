Dilution: the silent killer

Explorers and developers have no revenue, so every drill hole, study and permit is paid for with shareholders' money. Each raise issues new shares, usually at a discount. Your slice of any future mine shrinks with each one. Over a decade, a successful junior can easily issue several times its starting share count.

The ways juniors raise money (ASX)

Method How it works Watch for
Placement Shares sold to institutions and sophisticated investors, usually after a trading halt and bookbuild Discount to the last close; attaching options; lead-manager fees
SPP (share purchase plan) Retail holders can buy up to A$30,000 at the placement price Scale-backs when oversubscribed
Entitlement offer (rights issue) All holders can buy pro rata; renounceable or non-renounceable Fairer to retail. Deep discounts are common
Debt / project finance Bank loans secured on the project, usually after a DFS Hedging requirements, covenants, cost overrun facilities
Convertible notes Debt convertible into shares Conversion price resets ("toxic" structures)
Streams, royalties, offtake prepayments Selling future production for cash now Long-term NAV deduction (Module 19)

Placement capacity under LR 7.1 (15%) and LR 7.1A (10%, if approved) limits how many shares can be issued without a shareholder vote (Module 20).

The funding-gap waterfall

∑ Key formulaFunding gap

Gap = capex + contingency + working capital + ramp-up losses + interest during construction + corporate costs − debt capacity − cash on hand

  • Debt capacity for strong projects is often 50–60% of capex unverified.
  • Equity required = gap − debt.
  • New shares = equity required ÷ (current price × (1 − discount)).
◎ Worked exampleWorked: a developer's real NAV per share
  • Project NPV (unrisked) A$900m; cash A$80m; 800m shares at A$0.60 (market cap A$480m).
  • Remaining capex A$600m, with a 15% overrun allowance → A$690m.
  • Gap = 690 − 80 = A$610m. Debt at 50% → A$305m. Equity → A$305m.
  • Raise at a 15% discount: A$0.51 → 598m new shares (43% dilution).
  • Headline NAV/share = (900 + 80) ÷ 800 = A$1.23.
  • Fully funded NAV/share ≈ (900 + 80 − 90 overrun) ÷ 1,398 ≈ A$0.64.

The stock "trading at 0.49× NAV" is really trading near 0.94× fully funded NAV. The market is not as dumb as the headline P/NAV suggests.

✎ Your turnFaded

Same project, but debt covers only 30% of the gap and the raise is at a 25% discount. Compute new shares and fully funded NAV per share. (Equity ≈ A$427m at A$0.45 ≈ 949m shares → ≈A$0.51/share.)

Reading a placement release

Ask five questions:

  1. Discount to the last close (and to the 5-day VWAP). A discount of 10–15% is routine; more than 25% signals distress unverified.
  2. Size relative to market cap. More than 20% of market cap usually needs part of it approved at a meeting.
  3. Attaching options. Free options add hidden dilution.
  4. Who bought? Cornerstone institutions or strategic investors are a good signal. "Sophisticated investors" introduced by the lead manager are neutral.
  5. Use of funds. Does it fully fund the next milestone, or is another raise needed in six months?
▣ Case studyLiontown, Oct 2023 ✔

After Albemarle withdrew its A$3.00 proposal on 16 Oct 2023 (Hancock had built a 19.9% blocking stake), Liontown raised A$376m at A$1.80, a 35.5% discount to A$2.79. That was A$365m institutional plus A$10.8m from chairman Tim Goyder, plus an SPP of up to A$45m, alongside A$760m of debt. The shares fell about 35% on resumption. Takeover premium out, dilution in.

▣ Case studyHemi: raising on the good news ✔

De Grey announced a A$300m placement alongside its DFS on 28 Sep 2023. Raising into good news, while investors are excited, minimises the discount. It also reduced the "funding gap" discount the market applied to Hemi's NPV.

▣ Case studyBellevue, Apr 2025 ✔

A distressed raise: A$156.5m at 85c, a 25.8% discount, after a suspension and a second guidance cut. The shares fell as much as 26.6% that day.

Debt, ECAs and convertibles (one card)

Project finance typically needs a DFS, offtakes, hedging and a cost-overrun facility. Export credit agencies (ECAs) and government lenders such as Export Finance Australia and the NAIF increasingly fund critical minerals. Convertible notes are cheaper upfront but can become very dilutive if the share price falls and the conversion price resets.

✦ Hemi dossierPage 12: Hemi funding
  • DFS capex: A$1,345m. A$300m placement (Sep 2023).
  • Standalone funding plan: the funding gap after the placement would need a large debt package plus more equity. The standalone dilution risk was a key reason DEG traded below DFS NPV.
  • NST bid: funding risk disappears into NST's balance sheet, which is one source of synergy NST could pay away in the premium.
  • Exercise: use the funding widget with NPV A$2,900m, capex A$1,345m and an overrun of 20%, and compare the headline and fully funded figures.
⚑ Red flagFinancing red flags
  • Discounts above 25%, especially after a trading halt that follows bad news
  • Free attaching options with every raise
  • Convertible notes with price resets
  • Serial small raises that never fund the next milestone
  • Large lead-manager option packages
