Why DCF, not P/E?

A mine has a finite life, lumpy capex and a production profile that rises and falls. Earnings multiples miss all of that. A discounted cash flow model of each asset, summed into a net asset value (NAV), is the industry's primary valuation tool. Multiples are a cross-check (Module 18).

The asset DCF

∑ Key formulaAnnual cash flow

CF = price × payable production − operating costs − royalties − tax − capex (initial, sustaining, closure) ± working capital

NPV = Σ CFₜ ÷ (1 + r)ᵗ, often using mid-year discounting (t − 0.5).

Rules that matter:

  • Real vs nominal must match. If prices and costs are in today's dollars (real), use a real discount rate. Mixing nominal prices with a real rate overstates value.
  • Tax is calculated on taxable income after depreciation (Australia: 30% company tax), not on cash flow.
  • Closure costs at the end of life are real, and are often underestimated.
  • The valuation date matters. A study NPV at "start of construction" is not today's value if construction is two years away.
Go deeper: The discount rate optional

  • About 5% real for gold producers and low-risk assets in tier-1 jurisdictions.
  • 8% real is common for base metals and developers.
  • 10%+ for higher-risk jurisdictions or early-stage assets.

From asset NPV to corporate NAV

∑ Key formulaCorporate NAV

NAV = Σ (risked asset NPVs) + net cash (or − net debt) − PV(corporate costs) − hedge-book MTM ± other (investments, tax losses) + exploration value

NAV per share = NAV ÷ fully diluted shares (including in-the-money options and convertibles).

Stage risk-weighting

◆ Key ideaIllustrative risk factors unverified
Stage Risk factor on NPV
Scoping / PEA 0.2–0.3
PFS 0.3–0.5
DFS, unfunded 0.5–0.6
Funded / in construction 0.6–0.8
Steady-state production 0.9–1.0

Combine these with a funding adjustment (Module 14). An unfunded developer's equity is worth the risked NPV minus the dilution needed to build it.

These are DFS-cost figures. Cost inflation, a later FID (late FY27) and NST's redesign will reduce them. Apply your own AISC and capex factors, then a stage risk factor of about 0.6–0.7 for a permitted, funded but pre-FID project.

⚑ Red flagValuation red flags
  • Nominal prices with a real discount rate
  • Study NPVs quoted as "value today" years before construction
  • No closure costs or corporate costs
  • Spot prices used as a life-of-mine deck after a big rally
  • Undiluted share counts in NAV per share
